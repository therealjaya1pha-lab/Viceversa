In North Texas, many families raise their children in vibrant, dual-language environments. However, parents often receive conflicting advice regarding whether speaking two languages at home causes speech delays or confusion.

At **Vice Versa Speech and Language Services** (*¡Hablamos español!*), **Dana Beranger, MA, CCC-SLP** specializes in bilingual pediatric evaluation and intervention. Here, we clarify common myths with evidence-based clinical facts.

## Myth vs. Fact in Bilingual Child Development

### Myth 1: Speaking two languages causes speech delays.
**FACT:** Research shows that learning two languages simultaneously does *not* cause speech or language delays. Bilingual children reach major milestones (such as first words and word combinations) at the same overall age range as monolingual children when accounting for total vocabulary across both languages.

### Myth 2: "Code-switching" means the child is confused.
**FACT:** Code-switching — mixing words from Spanish and English in a single sentence (e.g., *"Quiero mas juice"*) — is a natural, sophisticated sign of bilingual cognitive development, not confusion.

### Myth 3: Parents should stop speaking their native language if their child has a delay.
**FACT:** Parents should always speak the language they feel most comfortable and natural using. High-quality rich linguistic input in a primary language provides a strong foundation for learning a secondary language at school.
