Mealtime should be a place of connection for families. However, for parents of children with severe food selectivity or sensory food aversions, breakfast, lunch, and dinner can turn into overwhelming daily struggles.

## Understanding the Difference: Picky Eaters vs. Problem Feeders

It's common for toddlers to go through picky phases, but problem feeders experience deeper sensory or oral-motor roadblocks.

| Characteristic | Picky Eater | Problem Feeder (Needs Therapy) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Food Selection** | Eats 30 or more different foods | Eats fewer than 20 total foods |
| **Response to New Foods** | Tolerates new food on plate | Cries, gags, or runs away if new food touches plate |
| **Food Groups** | Eats at least one food from each food group | Refuses entire food texture groups (e.g., all meats, all wet foods) |
| **Mealtime Behavior** | May complain but eats | Severe meltdowns, vomiting, or total refusal |

## What is the AEIOU Feeding Approach?

AEIOU stands for **Acceptance, Exposure, Independence, Oral Organization, and Utility**. This family-centered model respects the child's sensory threshold while gently expanding their palate:

1. **Acceptance:** Creating a calm environment where the child feels safe around food without pressure to consume.
2. **Exposure:** Systematic sensory exploration — looking, touching, smelling, licking, and eventually chewing new textures.
3. **Independence:** Empowering the child to use utensils and self-feed at their comfortable pace.
4. **Oral Organization:** Strengthening jaw stability, tongue tip elevation, and lateral tongue movements required for safe chewing.
5. **Utility:** Ensuring strategies transfer seamlessly to family meals, daycares, and schools.

## Tips for Parents at Home

- **Eliminate Mealtime Pressure:** Avoid forcing "just one bite". Instead, encourage your child to touch or kiss the new food item.
- **Offer Preferred & Non-Preferred Foods Together:** Always place at least one safe, preferred food item alongside a small sample of a new food.
- **Maintain Consistent Schedules:** Serve meals and structured snacks 2.5 to 3 hours apart to cultivate healthy hunger cues.
